Tools, Retrieval & Memory

A bare model can't check today's facts, do exact maths, or remember you. Tools, retrieval (RAG), and memory fix that — and each brings new responsibilities.

Part 1

Three blind spots of a bare model

Fix: search & APIsFrozen in time

Its knowledge stops at training. It can't know yesterday's news — but may still answer as if it does.

Fix: calculator, codeUnreliable maths

It predicts digits instead of computing them. 48,213 × 7,689 often comes out wrong.

Fix: memory storeForgets you

Each new conversation starts empty unless the app saves and re-supplies what matters.

Part 3

RAG: an open-book exam for AI

Retrieval-augmented generation finds the most relevant passages in trusted documents, hands them to the model, and asks it to answer only from them, with citations.

Step 1Chunk & index

Split documents into passages and store them for searching.

Step 2Retrieve

Find the passages most similar in meaning to the question.

Step 3Generate with citations

Answer from those passages only — or say "not in the sources".

Embeddings

Real systems compare meaning, not just words: text is turned into lists of numbers (embeddings) so "Can I get my money back?" matches a passage titled "Refunds". This demo uses simple word overlap so you can see the scoring; the developer lab uses real embeddings.

Part 4

Memory: short-term and long-term

Short-termThe context window

The conversation so far, re-sent with every message. Limited in size; gone when the chat ends.

Long-termA memory store

Facts the app saves (your name, preferences) and loads into later chats. Useful — and personal data with privacy duties.

Lab A · No code

Ground an AI in a real document

No-code lab · 25 min

NotebookLM and the Lakeview AI policy

NotebookLM answers only from sources you give it and shows citations. You'll test it with questions you can check — including ones the document can't answer.

notebooklm.google.comFree Google accountFictional policy, safe to share
  1. Get the source

    Open the Lakeview AI Use Policy and copy its text.

  2. Create a notebook

    In NotebookLM, create a notebook and add the text as a Copied text source.

  3. Ask three answerable questions
    Can a 12-year-old use the AI assistant at home? What must students include when they hand in work that used AI? Can AI be used to decide a student's grade?
    Expect: supervised lessons only under 13 · an AI-use statement · no. Click each citation and check the quoted passage.
  4. Ask one it can't answer

    What is the policy on students using AI to compose music? The document doesn't cover this. Does it say so, or invent an answer?

  5. Compare with no sources

    Ask the same first question to a general chatbot with no document. What changes?

Evidence for your portfolioA screenshot of one answer with its citation opened, and your notes on the unanswerable question.
Optional · 10 min

Memory Bot experiment

Find out what an assistant actually remembers — within a chat, and across chats.

  1. Share four facts

    Tell an assistant four made-up facts (a name, a subject, a hobby, a project). Never use real personal details.

  2. Test recall in the same chat

    Ask it to list all four.

  3. Open a new chat

    Ask what it knows about you. If it has a memory feature, check its settings: can you see and delete what it stored?

Responsible AI

Citations are a promise you can check

A citation lets anyone verify an answer in seconds — so check them, especially when the answer matters. And treat memory as personal data: an assistant that remembers you should let you see, correct, and delete what it stored.

Key terms

Tool callingThe model requests a function; the app runs it and returns the result.
RAGRetrieve relevant passages, then generate an answer grounded in them.
EmbeddingNumbers that represent the meaning of text, used for similarity search.
Vector databaseStores embeddings and finds the most similar ones quickly.
CitationA pointer to the exact source passage behind a claim.
Long-term memorySaved information reloaded into later conversations.
