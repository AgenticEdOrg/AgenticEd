Our Approach

Build it — and take responsibility for it

Most AI curricula teach students to use AI. AgenticEd teaches them to build agents and to govern them — the two skills the field actually hires for.

AgenticEd's distinctive contribution is a build-plus-govern pedagogy: every week pairs a hands-on building skill with a responsible-AI decision, so students graduate able to both create an agent and answer for what it does.

Why this is different

Introductory AI education usually stops at literacy — "here's what AI is, here's how to prompt it." Agent-building is rarely taught below the university level, and when it is, accountability is treated as a separate ethics unit bolted on at the end. AgenticEd integrates the two: governance isn't a final lecture, it's a thread woven through all six weeks, framed in plain language any beginner owns.

Principle 01
No code required
Students build real working agents with free no-code tools, so the barrier is curiosity, not programming.
Principle 02
Governance in every week
Accountability, harm, oversight, and ethics are practiced weekly — not saved for the end.
Principle 03
Access for every classroom
No-login, offline, and 45-minute paths mean firewalled and under-resourced schools can run it too.
Principle 04
Portfolio, not just completion
Each week produces one artifact that compounds into a college-application-ready capstone.

The responsible-AI thread, week by week

  1. Week 1 — "Who owns this agent's decisions?" plants accountability.
  2. Week 2 — Hallucination vs. misinformation: when is being wrong harmful?
  3. Week 3 — Grounding & retrieval: answering from real sources, not invention.
  4. Week 4 — Oversight by design: human-approval checkpoints in the pipeline.
  5. Week 5 — Demo Day's three accountability questions pay it all off.
  6. Week 6 — Ethics, safety, and careers in responsible AI.

Who it's for

Everyone. The course assumes no prior AI or coding experience, so it works for high-school classrooms (AP CS Principles, Intro to AI, electives), college students, career-switchers, self-learners, and community programs alike — with a deliberate focus on equity. The access design and the emphasis on responsible-AI oversight careers (not only engineering) are aimed at broadening who sees themselves in AI leadership, including learners from Title I and under-resourced schools.

Standards alignment
