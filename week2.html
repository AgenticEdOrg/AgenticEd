Part 1

How a chat model is built

After step 1 the model is a very good autocomplete. Steps 2 and 3 turn it into an assistant that follows requests, refuses some harmful ones, and writes in a helpful style. None of these steps gives it a database of verified facts.

Part 2

Tokens and next-word prediction

Models don't read letters or words — they read tokens: common words or pieces of rarer ones. Two sentences as split by OpenAI's current tokenizer (o200k_base):

Unbelievably, the tokenizer splits uncommon words into pieces.
The agent rerouted around the floodwater.

To write, the model scores every possible next token, picks one, adds it, and repeats. Temperature controls how adventurous the pick is. Try it:

Interactive

"The best way to learn a new skill is to …"

Low temperature: the top choice dominates (predictable). High: the long tail gets a chance (creative — and more likely to drift).

The context window

A model can only "see" a limited number of tokens at once — your instructions, the conversation, any documents, and its own reply. Anything outside that window is invisible to it, which is why long chats seem to forget their beginning.

Part 3

Why models are confidently wrong

A model produces text that fits the pattern of a good answer. Usually that's also true; sometimes it isn't, and it sounds exactly as confident either way. That's a hallucination.

MechanismHallucination

The model generates a plausible but false statement — an invented citation, date, or rule.

HarmMisinformation

Someone believes or acts on it. A fake fun fact is harmless; a fake medicine dose is not.

Hallucinations are most likely for rare or recent facts, precise details (numbers, quotes, references), and questions about things that don't exist. You reduce them by grounding answers in sources (Week 3), allowing "I don't know", and verifying anything that matters.

Part 4

Prompts that work

PartWhat it doesExample
RoleSets expertise and voice"You are a patient chemistry teacher."
TaskSays exactly what to do"Explain why ice floats."
ContextAudience and background"For a 13-year-old who knows what density is."
ConstraintsLimits"Under 100 words. No equations."
FormatShape of the answer"Two short paragraphs, then one check question."

Few-shot prompting adds 1–3 worked examples so the model copies your pattern. Step-by-step ("think it through, then give the answer on the last line") helps with multi-step problems.

Responsible AI

Wrong is not the same as harmful

The skill isn't spotting that AI can be wrong — it's knowing when being wrong matters. Before acting on an answer, ask: what happens if this is false? Low stakes: read critically. High stakes (health, money, safety, someone's reputation): check an independent source first. Asking the same AI "are you sure?" is not a check.

Key terms

TokenThe unit of text a model reads and writes — a word or word-piece.
TemperatureHow random the model's next-token choice is.
Context windowHow much text the model can consider at once.
HallucinationConfident output that is false or unsupported.
Few-shotIncluding examples in the prompt so the model copies the pattern.
System promptBackground instructions that set an assistant's role and rules.
